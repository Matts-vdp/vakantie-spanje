The signature walk of the Picos, and the reason to leave early. The path was cut in the 1940s to service a hydroelectric channel and runs for 12 km through a canyon 1,500 m deep, carved 200 m above the river with tunnels and overhangs.

Special access plan on 24 SeptemberPark in Arenas de Cabrales and use the prebookable bus to Poncebos. Allow roughly 30 minutes each way on top of the drive, and recheck current trail notices before leaving.

From Poncebos the first 30 minutes climb hard out of the valley; after that the path levels and the gorge closes in around you. Turn back at the two-hour mark wherever you are. Treat that as a rule rather than a target — the return leg is the same length and the light goes early in the canyon.

No water, no shade, no fenceCarry 1.5 litres each. The drop is unguarded in places and the surface is loose in others; it is not exposed climbing, but it is not a promenade. Goats dislodge stones from above.

On the way home: Arenas de Cabrales, where the cheese caves are open to visitors. Cabrales is ripened in natural limestone caves in the mountainside — cold, wet, and thick with the smell of it. Buy a wedge; it travels badly in a warm car, so make it the last stop.

Las Médulas is what remains after Roman engineers dismantled a mountain range for its gold. The technique — ruina montium — meant driving tunnel networks into the rock, then releasing aqueduct water into them until the hydraulic pressure brought the whole hillside down. Pliny the Elder saw it and wrote that the engineers themselves were frightened by what they had made. What is left is a landscape of red pinnacles.

The fire, honestlyAugust 2025 burned the chestnut groves that frame the site, and around 40% of them are rated poorly for medium-term recovery. But the CSIC assessment found fewer than 20% of individual chestnuts badly damaged, described the heritage damage as isolated and reversible, and named soil erosion as the main problem; the report sent to UNESCO in April 2026 says the same. Orellán reopened within two months of the fire. The pinnacles are geology and were never at risk.

The live issue is trail closures from rockfall risk where erosion has destabilised slopes. Which paths are open changes — ring the Casa del Parque the week before you travel. Go to the Mirador de Orellán for the late-afternoon light regardless; that is when the rock turns properly red.

You are sleeping in Orellán — the village the viewpoint belongs to. That is the whole point of this base: you walk up for the evening light rather than driving to it, and again at dawn tomorrow if the sky looks worth it.

Details, options & background

Check out and drive about half an hour down the Teverga valley to Villanueva. Las Xanas sits directly on the road toward Oviedo, which is the whole reason the schedule works — you are not doubling back on anything today.

The Desfiladero de las Xanas is a path barely a metre wide, chiselled into the wall of a limestone cleft in the 1920s to connect a village to its valley. It climbs about 2.5 km to the meadows at Pedroveya with the rock closing to a few metres overhead. It takes its name from the xanas, the water spirits of Asturian folklore, who are said to live in exactly this sort of place and to be less benign than they look.

The official circular route is 9.03 km with 437 m of ascent, rated medium and estimated at 3–5 hours before a sit-down lunch. It is one of central Asturias's best short walks, but not the compact 3h30-with-lunch stop the earlier draft assumed.

If it's rainingCueva Huerta does not normally run public weekday visits after 15 September, and Banduxu's steep access road is not a severe-weather solution. The reliable options are the Prehistory Park at Teverga or the Museum of Fine Arts in central Oviedo.

The culture version. Go straight to Santa María del Naranco and San Miguel de Lillo: 9th-century Asturian pre-Romanesque buildings from the period when this small kingdom was the only unconquered Christian territory in Iberia. Naranco began as a royal hall rather than a church, and it looks it. They stand only about 200 m apart, not twenty minutes apart, and both belong to the UNESCO listing.

The choice that fixes the dayWinter hours begin on 1 October and Naranco closes at 14:30. Visit before roughly 13:45, or walk Las Xanas and skip the interiors. Do not plan both.

Then Oviedo, in the afternoon rather than overnight. An 11:00 flight means leaving your bed at 08:30 tomorrow, so there was never going to be a morning in the city — better to take it now while everything is open. The cathedral and its Cámara Santa, a pre-Romanesque chamber swallowed by the later Gothic building and holding the Cruz de la Victoria; then the Plaza del Fontán and the squares around it. The centre is compact and improbably well-scrubbed; two or three hours covers it.

Oviedo's afternoon windowOn Thursday 1 October, cultural access to the cathedral and Cámara Santa is currently 10:00–13:00 and 16:00–18:00. Build the afternoon around the 16:00 reopening and arrive well before the final admission.
Calle Gascona before you goThe cider street, and its bars open from lunchtime — so you get your culín in daylight instead of at midnight. Asturian sidra natural is unfiltered and still, and is poured escanciada from arm's length above the glass to aerate it on impact. Two fingers deep, drunk in one go before it flattens; the last drop goes on the floor to rinse the glass. Let the waiter pour — everyone tries it themselves once and everyone wears it. Whoever is driving to Avilés sits this one out.

Thirty minutes on to Avilés. A working estuary town rather than a cathedral city, and the only place on this route that isn't limestone and Romanesque. Calle Galiana is an arcaded medieval street running down to the old quarter, and the streets around Plaza de España are compact and almost entirely untouristed. Across the water sits the Centro Niemeyer — Oscar Niemeyer's only work in Spain, white curves dropped into a post-industrial estuary. Lit up after dark it is worth the ten-minute walk even if you don't go in.

At the hotelPalacio de Avilés is a 17th-century palace on Plaza de España with a modern wing attached, and guests report direct access from the hotel through to the car park and the Ferrera gardens. Two things to specify when booking: ask for a room in the modern wing, because the old palace rooms are inconsistent and one guest found theirs dated with a leaking shower — and ask whether an event is booked. It hosts weddings, and one guest lost a night to music running until five. A Thursday in October is low risk, but it is a thirty-second question the night before a flight.

Before you go

It will rain

Late September on the Cantabrian coast runs 18–21 °C and wet; the mountains are cooler and wetter. Assume two of your five walking days are compromised. The three Potes days are fully interchangeable — use the clearest for Fuente Dé, the next for the Cares.

The driving, honestly

Roughly 1,300 km before optional side trips, rising toward 1,600 km if you use the driving-heavy León and reserve options. The hairpin passes are gone. One mountain road remains — San Glorio on Day 6, broad and well-graded. The longest continuous stint anywhere in the twelve days is about two hours, on Day 10. Everything else is under 90 minutes. Budget 20% more than the satnav suggests on the N-roads and don't drive the Hermida gorge after dark.

What you gave up

The guided bear observation. Late September sits past the best window and one session was roughly a coin flip. Dropping it removed a two-hour backtrack. Somiedo itself then went with the hairpin roads, so the western mountains are off this trip altogether — the price of never driving a pass you did not want to drive. Marabio still offers high pasture and herders' cabins, but it now sits in reserve because the long Senda del Oso version takes most or all of Day 11.

Pack swimwear anyway

Not a beach trip, but the Cantabrian sea peaks in late September at around 19–20 °C — warmer than July, because the water lags the air. The Llanes coast has the clearest openings, while Salinas is possible from Avilés on the final afternoon. Keep a towel somewhere reachable rather than at the bottom of a case.

Where the slack is

Three things are deliberately loose: the León Saturday, the three Cosgaya days, which are fully interchangeable, and the Bierzo day — Las Médulas on foot, or Peñalba, or neither. If weather wrecks the Cares or Fuente Dé, that is where the slack lives. From Day 10 onward the route is shaped by hotel arrival, the bike shuttle, museum hours and the flight. Day 11 is now intended as a substantial ride, so Marabio is an alternative rather than a guaranteed afternoon extra.

Carry cash

Carry some small notes for rural restaurants, cave visits and parking, but confirm rather than assume each operator's payment rules. Bike companies differ, and Covadonga parking and transport should be booked or paid through the current official channels where possible.

One last thing

Everything time-sensitive here — access regulations, cable car operations, cave calendars, trail closures at Las Médulas — should be confirmed once now and again in the last week before you fly. The Covadonga regulation in particular runs to 18 October, so it applies to you and there is no version of that day where you drive up yourself.
